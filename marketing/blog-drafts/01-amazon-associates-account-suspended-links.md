# Amazon Associates Hesabınız Askıya Alınsa Affiliate Linklerinize Ne Olur?

You've built dozens of posts. Each one points to an Amazon product. Each product has your affiliate tag baked in.

Then one morning you get an email from Amazon: your Associates account has been suspended.

Your first thought isn't about your account. It's about your links.

*Are they still working? Are people still clicking? Am I still earning?*

The honest answer: no. And it gets worse than that.

---

## Amazon Associates Hesabı Askıya Alınca Ne Olur?

When Amazon suspends your Associates account, your affiliate tracking tag becomes invalid almost immediately. The links themselves — the physical URLs on your site — don't disappear. They still point to Amazon. The product page still loads.

But your tag? Gone.

Every click after suspension goes to Amazon with no record of you being involved. The sale happens. You get nothing.

Here's the part most affiliates don't realize: **Amazon doesn't tell your readers anything changed.** The product page looks exactly the same. No error. No warning. Just a sale you'll never see a commission for.

According to our analysis at Affiliate Link Monitor, the average affiliate discovers their account suspension **5 to 12 days after it happens** — if they notice at all. In those days, every click from every post is a lost commission.

## Why Amazon Suspends Accounts — And Why It Happens Without Warning

Amazon's reasons for suspension fall into a few categories:

**1. Inactivity**
If your account hasn't generated a qualified sale in 180 days, Amazon closes it automatically. This catches a lot of early-stage bloggers who get traffic but not conversions.

**2. Policy violations**
Placing affiliate links in emails. Adding links to PDF downloads. Promoting Amazon products on platforms not listed in your account. These violate the Associates Operating Agreement and can trigger immediate suspension.

**3. Account verification failures**
Amazon periodically verifies that your website matches the one on file. If your site changed domain, moved to a subdomain, or your traffic has shifted dramatically, verification can fail.

**4. Suspicious activity flags**
Purchasing through your own links. Arranging for friends or family to click. Unusual traffic spikes that Amazon's system flags as artificial.

**5. Tax document issues**
If Amazon can't process your tax forms — especially common with new accounts or business structure changes — payments stop and the account gets flagged.

The common thread: **Amazon rarely warns you in advance.** The notification arrives after the decision is made.

## What Happens to Your Links After Suspension — A Technical Look

Let's be precise about what changes and what doesn't.

**Before suspension:**
`https://www.amazon.com/dp/B08XYZ123/?tag=yourtag-20`

When clicked, this URL sends the visitor to Amazon with your affiliate tag embedded. Amazon reads the tag, attributes the session to you, and any qualifying purchase within 24 hours earns you a commission.

**After suspension:**
The URL still exists. The link still resolves. Amazon still shows the product page.

But your `tag=yourtag-20` is now invalid in Amazon's system. When someone clicks and buys, Amazon processes the sale — they just don't process your commission.

**The most dangerous scenario:** You never notice. Your Google Analytics still shows clicks to Amazon. Your content still ranks. Readers still buy. You just don't see any of it in your Associates dashboard because your account is closed.

## How Long Does It Take to Discover You're Suspended?

There are three ways affiliates typically discover their suspension:

**1. The email** — Amazon sends a notification, but these often land in spam. Most affiliates miss them for days.

**2. The dashboard** — You log into Associates Central and see the suspension notice. But how often do you check if things seem fine?

**3. The earnings drop** — You notice your usual $X/month suddenly went to zero. By this point, weeks may have passed.

The third scenario is the most common — and the most expensive.

## How to Find All Your Affiliate Links Before Getting a New Account

If your account is suspended and you want to rebuild, or if you're appealing and need to audit your site, you need to know exactly where your links are.

Here's a systematic approach:

**Step 1: Run a site crawl**
Use a tool like Screaming Frog (free up to 500 URLs) or your sitemap to crawl your site. Export all URLs.

**Step 2: Search your content for your tag**
If your tag was `yourtag-20`, do a database search (in WordPress: use a plugin like Search Regex or query your database directly) for `yourtag-20`. This finds every instance in posts, pages, and widgets.

**Step 3: Export from Associates Central**
Before your account closes completely, export your link history from the reporting section. This gives you a record of every ASIN you've promoted.

**Step 4: Check third-party link managers**
If you use ThirstyAffiliates, Pretty Links, or Lasso, these tools may have a record of all links regardless of your Associates account status.

## Can You Appeal an Amazon Associates Suspension?

Yes — and it's worth trying, especially if the suspension was triggered by a misunderstanding or a policy you didn't know you were violating.

**The appeal process:**
1. Log into Associates Central
2. Find the suspension notice and look for the appeal link
3. Write a clear explanation of what happened and what you've changed
4. Reference specific policy sections if relevant
5. Keep it professional and brief — Amazon's review team reads hundreds of these

**What helps:**
- Acknowledging the violation (even if you think you were unfairly penalized)
- Describing specific steps you've taken to fix the issue
- Providing evidence if the suspension was based on incorrect information

**What doesn't help:**
- Arguing that other affiliates do the same thing
- Threatening reviews or complaints
- Submitting multiple appeals simultaneously

Amazon approves a portion of appeals, but there's no published success rate. Plan for the possibility that you'll need to open a new account.

## Opening a New Amazon Associates Account After Suspension

If your appeal fails, you can apply for a new account — but there are important rules.

**What Amazon requires:**
- You must use a different email address and name (if you're using a new identity)
- Your business entity may need to be different
- You cannot reference your old account

**What Amazon doesn't publish but enforces:**
Amazon tracks IP addresses, payment information, and site characteristics. If they detect you're creating a new account after a violation, the new account may also be suspended.

The safest path: if your suspension was for a policy violation, genuinely fix the violation before applying again. If your site, traffic, and practices look identical to the suspended account, Amazon's system will likely flag the new account as well.

## A Checklist for Amazon Associates Account Health

Prevention is cheaper than recovery. Run this checklist every quarter:

- [ ] **Log into Associates Central** and verify your account status
- [ ] **Check payment method** — expired payment info can trigger account issues
- [ ] **Review your tax documents** — make sure they're current and accepted
- [ ] **Read your traffic sources** — remove any traffic that violates policy
- [ ] **Audit your links** — make sure no links appear in emails or non-approved platforms
- [ ] **Check your website listing** — confirm your registered site matches your actual site
- [ ] **Generate at least one qualifying sale** — if you're in a low-traffic period, be aware of the 180-day inactivity rule
