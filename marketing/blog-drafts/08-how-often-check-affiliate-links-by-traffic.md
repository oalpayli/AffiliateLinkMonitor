# Amazon Affiliate Linklerinizi Kaç Saitte Bir Kontrol Etmelisiniz? (Trafik Bazında Rehber)

"Check your affiliate links regularly" is advice that appears in every affiliate marketing guide. But nobody defines "regularly."

Monthly? Weekly? Daily? Continuously?

The honest answer: it depends on your traffic. And the cost of getting it wrong scales directly with the traffic you're sending.

This guide breaks down monitoring frequency by traffic tier, with specific recommendations for each level.

---

## Why Traffic Volume Determines Monitoring Frequency

Every hour a broken affiliate link goes undetected, some number of clicks happen with no commission earned. Whether that's 2 clicks or 500 depends on your traffic.

**The math:**
If your site sends 50 clicks/day to an affiliate link:
- Link broken for 1 hour: ~2 clicks lost = minimal
- Link broken for 1 week: ~350 clicks lost = significant

If your site sends 500 clicks/day to an affiliate link:
- Link broken for 1 hour: ~21 clicks lost = meaningful
- Link broken for 1 week: ~3,500 clicks lost = potentially hundreds of dollars

The same link failure has dramatically different consequences depending on traffic volume. Your monitoring frequency should match your exposure.

---

## Tier 1: Under 1,000 Monthly Visitors

**Who this is:** New and early-stage affiliate sites. Content is building. Traffic is beginning to arrive, but individual posts see tens, not hundreds, of clicks per month.

**Your exposure per broken link:** Low. An out-of-stock link at this traffic level costs perhaps $5-20 before you'd naturally notice during content review.

**Recommended frequency:** Weekly manual check

**What to check weekly:**
- Click every Amazon affiliate link in your five highest-traffic posts
- Confirm the linked products are available ("Add to Cart" visible)
- Verify prices match any price claims in your content
- Confirm your affiliate tag is in the URL

**Time requirement:** 15-30 minutes per week

**Tools to use:** Manual browser check + a free link monitor covering your 10 most important links

At this traffic level, a free monitoring plan (10 links, daily scans) covers your most important links automatically. Manual checks fill in the gaps.

---

## Tier 2: 1,000 to 10,000 Monthly Visitors

**Who this is:** Growing sites with consistent content and active publishing. Individual posts may generate hundreds of clicks per month. Affiliate income is meaningful — perhaps $200-$1,500/month.

**Your exposure per broken link:** Moderate to significant. A top post with a broken link for a week could cost $30-150 in lost commissions.

**Recommended frequency:** Daily automated scans + monthly manual audit

**Daily automated monitoring should cover:**
- Your top 20-30 affiliate links by traffic
- Products in high-turnover categories (electronics, seasonal, trending)
- Links in posts that rank in the top 10 for their target keywords

**Monthly manual audit should include:**
- All affiliate links across your site (not just top performers)
- Cross-referencing Associates Central clicks against your site analytics
- Checking that product descriptions in your content still match the actual product

**Time requirement:** 5 hours per month (1 monthly audit + monitoring setup)

**Tools to use:** A monitoring tool covering your top 30 links on daily scans. At $0-12/month, this is one of the highest-ROI tools for a site at this traffic level.

---

## Tier 3: 10,000 to 50,000 Monthly Visitors

**Who this is:** Established affiliate sites with significant organic presence. Multiple posts ranking for competitive keywords. Affiliate income may range from $1,500-$7,000/month.

**Your exposure per broken link:** High. At this traffic level, a broken link in a top post for just 48 hours can cost $50-300 in lost commissions. A week of undetected failure could mean $200-$1,000 gone.

**Recommended frequency:** Hourly scans on top links + weekly manual spot-check

**Hourly monitoring should cover:**
- Your top 50-60 affiliate links
- All links in posts with >500 monthly clicks
- Links to products in high-volatility categories
- Any links you've recently added or updated

**Weekly spot-check should verify:**
- Random sample of 10 links from across your catalog
- Any posts receiving unusual traffic spikes (check links in those posts first)
- Links that monitoring flagged but you haven't investigated yet

**Time requirement:** 1-2 hours per week (mostly review of monitoring alerts)

**Tools to use:** Pro monitoring plan with hourly scans. At this traffic level, a month of lost commissions from a single undetected broken link can easily exceed a year of Pro subscription costs.

**Key argument for hourly monitoring:** Traffic spikes are unpredictable. A post might suddenly receive significant social or search traffic, driving 10x its normal clicks. If that post's link broke the night before the spike, you'd lose a large commission window before daily scans would catch it.

---

## Tier 4: 50,000+ Monthly Visitors

**Who this is:** High-authority affiliate sites, niche site operators, content agencies managing multiple sites. Affiliate income likely exceeds $5,000-$10,000+/month.

**Your exposure per broken link:** Severe. At this traffic level, a broken link in a top 5 post can cost $500-$2,000+ per week.

**Recommended frequency:** Continuous (or near-continuous) monitoring across full link catalog

**What "continuous monitoring" means in practice:**
- All affiliate links scanned hourly or more frequently
- Immediate email alerts for any link status change
- Separate alert priority for top 20 links (highest urgency)
- Integration with team communication tools if you have staff

**Process additions at this level:**
- Assign a team member to monitor alerts and respond within 2 hours during business hours
- Maintain a "backup link" document — for each top affiliate link, have an approved alternative link ready to swap
- Implement post-level revenue tracking so you can identify which post's link failure caused a commission drop

**Time requirement:** 2-4 hours per week (mostly alert response and post-incident review)

**Tools to use:** Pro monitoring (or enterprise affiliate management platforms) covering your full link catalog.

## The "High-Value Link" Rule Regardless of Traffic Tier

Regardless of where you fall in the traffic tiers above, there's a universal rule: **your highest-earning links deserve more frequent monitoring than the rest.**

Every affiliate site has a power law distribution: 20% of links generate 80% of commissions. Identify your top 10-20 links by commission generated (visible in Associates Central Earnings by ASIN report). These links should always be on your highest available scan frequency.

If you're on the Free plan and can only actively monitor 10 links, those 10 should be your highest-earning links — not necessarily your highest-traffic posts (these can differ).

## The Cost of Under-Monitoring vs. Over-Monitoring

There's a tendency to avoid monitoring tools because of the cost. Let's put this in perspective.

**Scenario: 5,000 monthly visitors, 3% affiliate conversion, $20 average commission**
Estimated monthly affiliate income: ~$3,000

**Cost of 2 weeks of undetected broken link on top post:**
~$150-400 in lost commissions (conservative estimate)

**Cost of Pro monitoring plan:**
$12/month

The math is straightforward. At almost any meaningful traffic level, the cost of under-monitoring exceeds the cost of monitoring tools by a factor of 10 or more.

The reason people don't monitor isn't economics — it's that commission losses are invisible until you look for them. Nobody sends you an invoice for the commissions you didn't earn.

## Related: What to Do Between Checks

Monitoring tells you when something breaks. But there are proactive steps to reduce breakage frequency between checks:

- Update old posts that reference specific prices — prices change, and inaccurate content has higher abandonment rates regardless of link health
- Replace products that have been out of stock for 30+ days — they're unlikely to return
- Diversify your product recommendations — if one product goes out of stock, readers have alternatives within your content
- Use link managers that allow single-point updates — so when a product changes, one edit fixes all instances
